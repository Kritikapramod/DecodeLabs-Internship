What is phishing?

Fraudulent messages that imitate trusted senders to steal credentials, money or data.

Common techniques

Fake urgency, brand impersonation, lookalike domains, shortened links, fake prizes, advance-fee requests.

Spotting suspicious emails

Check the sender address, generic greetings, pressure to act, spelling oddities and unexpected attachments.

Inspecting URLs safely

Read the domain just before the first single slash. Hover to preview links. Type known addresses yourself. Never open unknown links to "see what happens".

Never share OTPs or passwords

Banks and services never ask for them by message or call. An OTP approves an action on your account.

After clicking a bad link

Disconnect if a file downloaded, change passwords from a clean device, enable 2FA, tell your bank and IT team, watch your accounts.

Reporting phishing

Use your email provider's "report phishing", forward to your organisation's security team, and report to your national cyber-crime portal.

About PhishGuard

Built as a cybersecurity internship project (DecodeLabs). The backend is a transparent rule-based engine in Python's standard library: phrase rules, local URL heuristics, social-engineering mapping and an explainable score. It uses no machine learning, no external APIs, and never visits submitted URLs.

Scores are heuristic assessments, not guarantees. Limitations: English-centric rules, no sender/header analysis, small brand list.
